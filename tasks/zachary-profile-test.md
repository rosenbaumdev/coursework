## Instrument Retro (pilot notes — separate from student data)

**What worked:**
The iterative narrowing on the workout/lifting thread worked well — starting at "what are you into" and following the energy down to a specific product frustration and a specific feature request happened naturally without forcing it. The "who else has this problem" question was the right move for audience validation and he answered it immediately. Rapid-fire systems literacy map was clean and fast — good calibration tool that didn't feel like a test.

**What didn't work or was awkward:**
The structure/check-ins question sequence got confused when Zachary revealed he thought we'd be working together throughout the course. The interview had to do some real-time expectation management that probably should be handled upstream in course framing before the intake interview begins — it's not the intake's job to explain the course architecture, and doing it mid-interview broke flow.

The "does a tighter schedule help or make it worse" question was also slightly off — he correctly pointed out that his dad sets that, not him. The question was useful for data but the framing put him in a decision-making role he doesn't have. Reframe as clearly observational ("just so we know how you work") from the start.

**Gaps in coverage:**
Feedback sensitivity wasn't probed at all — no data on how he handles criticism or being told something he built isn't working. Would be useful to have a lightweight question for this in future versions.

No probe on what he's tried before that didn't stick — there's likely more completion history (school projects, hobbies, etc.) that would give a richer picture of where the boredom pattern kicks in and why.

**Format note:**
Transcript length felt appropriate for the depth of profile generated. The two-question close ("anything left?" + summary playback) worked well — the summary playback in particular is a high-value instrument move that should stay in all versions.
