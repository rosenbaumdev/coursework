## Instrument Retro *(pilot notes — separate from student data)*

**What worked:**
- The rapid-fire systems literacy scan worked well. Quick to administer, gave clear signal, and Zachary answered honestly rather than performing knowledge he doesn't have. Worth keeping.
- Asking "say more about that" on the AI-is-the-future answer surfaced the real, grounded reason (his and his dad's actual usage) almost immediately. The follow-up discipline paid off.
- The investing translator idea only emerged after a deliberate "do you see anything missing?" reframe. The prior open-ended "is there anything that bugs you?" got nothing. That reframe is worth baking into the instrument as a standard prompt — it's more generative.
- The mid-interview moment where Zachary corrected himself ("I meant to say impress colleges as well as money") suggests the conversational, non-quiz framing is working — he felt free to revise.

**What didn't work:**
- There was a significant error mid-interview: the interviewer offered three venture directions (AI form-check coach, lifting-program generator, gym-meme brand) that had no connection to anything Zachary had said. He called it out plainly — "None to be honest." The instrument needs a hard guardrail: never introduce example directions that aren't sourced directly from the student's own words. This one got caught, but it could have anchored him to something irrelevant.
- The finishing/completion question could be sharpened. "Do you tend to finish things or drift?" is fine, but the follow-up that got real signal ("what tips it one way or the other?") is actually the useful question. Consider leading with that.
- The session never got a clean picture of his social context beyond "I know people into it." A single follow-up on that (who are these people, how do you talk about this with them?) could matter a lot for product-market thinking — he could be his own first test user group.
- No specific question on what he's actually seen his dad build or do with AI beyond "email summarizes text summaries." That's a potentially rich thread — the dad is a real, proximate model — that the instrument didn't pursue.
- The logistics section came late and ran a bit loose. The capital question got a usable answer ("I could invest / unsure on amount") but the Venmo/PayPal confirmation was buried. Consider moving logistics earlier or making it its own tight block.
